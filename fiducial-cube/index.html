Papercraft pattern · one sheet · 36 mm cube

Base Fiducial Cube

The sketched checkerboard fiducial, unfolded into a cut-and-glue net. Every face carries the same 3×3 checker (12 mm cells). Color the gray squares, score the dashed lines, cut the solid outline, glue the hatched tabs.

  1. Print

    Print at 100% / “Actual size” — don’t let the dialog shrink it. The sheet fits A5, A4, and US Letter. Cardstock (160–250 gsm) makes a sturdier block; plain paper works too.

    Want a bigger cube? On A4/Letter choose “Fit to page” instead — you’ll get a ≈53 mm cube, and every step below is scale-free.

  2. Color

    Fill every gray square solid black with a broad marker, crayon, or paint — the sketch’s “black & white paper” idea, in ink. Work right up to the cell edges: crisp black/white boundaries are what a camera locks onto. Leave the white squares and the tabs clean.

  3. Score

    Before cutting, run a scoring tool (empty ballpoint, the back of a butter knife, a bone folder) along every dashed line against a ruler. Scoring while the sheet is still whole is far easier than after.

  4. Cut

    Cut along the solid outline only. Dashed lines are never cut.

  5. Fold

    Crease every dashed line away from the printed side, so the pattern ends up on the outside of the cube. Sharpen each crease fully before gluing.

  6. Glue

    Thin layer of glue stick or PVA on the hatched side of each tab.

    a. Roll the vertical strip of four faces into a square tube and glue its single bottom tab inside the open edge of the top face.

    b. Cap each end: glue the three tabs of a side face, fold the tabs inward, fold the cap flush, and press its tabs against the inside walls for a few seconds. Done — one 36 mm fiducial block.

From the sketch

No printer at all? The original idea works as drawn: cut 12 mm squares of black paper and glue five per face, checker-wise, onto a white 36 mm cube.

Blocks. Print several sheets and stack the cubes — the checker pattern continues across a stack, like the tower in the sketch.

Telling faces apart. All six faces are identical, which is fine for detection. If you need to know which face is up (pose tracking), ink a small mark into a white corner cell of one or two faces.
