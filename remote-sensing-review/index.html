Review · sensing across scale

One Estimator,
Thirty Orders of Magnitude

A review of the sensing work in the cwervo/playground repository, and of the claim it keeps re-proving at hobby scale: that from molecular absorption to interferometric astronomy, the physics of the encoding changes completely while the estimator, the calibration discipline, and the bounds that limit both stay the same.

Written for readers in neuropsychology, spectroscopy, and X-ray / laser / IR interferometry for art conservation.
Source corpus: 21 sensing-related programs, C/Tcl/JS, 2024–2026.

What this document is, exactly.

Built marks a claim backed by code and test suites in this repository: camera/projector computer vision, a steganographic channel, and a print-and-rescan codec that recovers a program byte-exact from a photograph of a piece of paper.

Argued marks the extension outward. This repository contains no glucose sensor, no satellite pipeline, no interferometer. The rungs of the ladder below at those scales are drawn from the published literature and are presented as argument, not as work performed here. The value of the small artifacts is that they are complete end-to-end sensing chains you can hold in one hand and read in an afternoon, which makes the invariants unusually easy to see.

01  /  The invariant chain

Every instrument in this review is the same six stages

A sensing instrument couples a state you care about onto a physical field, lets that field propagate through a medium that degrades it, collects some fraction of it through a finite aperture, quantises what arrives, and inverts. The interesting engineering claim is how little of that chain is scale-dependent. The coupling — the cross-section, the transition, the scattering mechanism — is entirely specific to the scale and to the band. Everything downstream of it is shared, and shared not by analogy but by literal identity of operator: the same normalised cross-correlation, the same phase unwrapping, the same reference-standard calibration, the same information-theoretic ceiling.

02  /  The corpus

Six small instruments, and the general principle each one is a special case of

These are hobby programs written for a projector-and-camera table. Their interest here is that each is a complete chain, small enough that the invariant is not obscured by engineering, and that several of them fail in instructive ways.

opencv/OpenCVThreshold.folk  Built

Adaptive thresholding on live camera slices: the binarisation decision is made against a local neighbourhood mean rather than a global constant, and the last ten frames are retained in a ring buffer.

The general case. The illumination field is non-stationary, so the sufficient statistic is local. This is flat-fielding, and it is the same correction as dividing a microscope frame by a blank, or normalising a reflectance cube against a Spectralon reference tile. A global threshold on a non-uniformly illuminated field does not lose precision; it manufactures structure that is not in the object.

templateMatcher/templateMatcher.cpp  Built

Locates a stored patch in a camera frame using OpenCV TM_CCORR_NORMED, normalising the response surface and taking its argmax.

The general case. This is the matched filter, provably the optimal detector for a known signal in additive white Gaussian noise, and the normalisation is what buys invariance to illumination gain. The identical operator is radar pulse compression, the correlator at the heart of VLBI, fringe-order tracking in a Michelson, and the cross-correlation step in digital image correlation used to map strain in a stressed panel. One operator, four literatures.

folk/blob/blob.folk  Built

Extracts an arbitrary polygonal region from a camera frame given the camera intrinsics, the projector intrinsics, and the page's quad — then runs blob detection inside it.

The general case. Recovering object coordinates from sensor coordinates through a calibrated projective model. Orthorectification of a satellite scene is this same computation with a different camera model and a terrain height field; photogrammetric documentation of a painting's surface is this with a metric scale bar in frame. Geometric calibration and radiometric calibration are separate problems and stay separate at every scale.

folk_qr_demos/{generator,scanner}.folk  Built

Vendored Nayuki qrcodegen plus a scanner, bootstrapped into the runtime as base64 payloads.

The general case. A QR code is a two-dimensional channel code with Reed–Solomon parity sized to an announced error-correction level, plus three finder patterns that solve pose and pitch before any data bit is read. Acquisition precedes demodulation. Every instrument in this review has a finder-pattern equivalent, and the ones that do not have to be told their scale by a human, which is where systematic error enters.

smokesignal/index.html  Built

Hides a payload in the least-significant bits of a procedurally generated smoke plume (fractal value noise with domain warping), across a selectable colour model — luma only, RGB, or CMYK — with a magic-number header, a length field, and a decoder that identifies the colour model by trial decode.

The general case, and an instructive failure. Capacity scales linearly with the number of bit planes used, and imperceptibility scales inversely: the tool exposes the trade-off as a slider, which is honest. But the payload dies the moment the carrier is JPEG-compressed, because the LSB plane is exactly what a perceptual codec is licensed to discard. The code was matched to the image, not to the channel. This is the single most transferable lesson in the corpus, and it is the same error as choosing an integration time without reference to the noise spectrum of the detector.

PrintablePrograms.png/  Built

The centrepiece: a Tcl program encoded into a band of colour cells around a printed page, such that a photograph of the printed page decodes back to the byte-exact source. The test suite verifies recovery after an 80% rescale and JPEG quality-82 recompression.

The general case. This is a designed physical channel, and it independently reinvents most of the calibration discipline of a conservation imaging protocol. It is worth unpacking in full.

Four decisions in that frame deserve to be named, because each has a direct counterpart in instrument practice. The calibration strip means the decoder never assumes it knows what the printer's cyan is; it measures it, on this print, under this illumination. That is the argument for putting a reference standard in frame rather than trusting a factory characterisation. The self-encoded geometry makes cell-count recovery immune to pitch measurement error at any scale, which is the argument for a specimen that carries its own scale bar. The CRC-guarded packet repeated around the ring is a repetition code sized to an estimated channel error rate, with the decoder accepting the first copy that verifies. And the choice to defer deskew and perspective correction to a separate vision layer keeps rectification and demodulation independently testable.

The failure mode the corpus keeps demonstrating is not insufficient resolution. It is a code chosen without reference to the channel it must survive.

03  /  The ladder

The same argument at every rung, from analyte to source

What follows is the extension outward. Argued throughout: this is the published state of each field, organised so that the recurrence is visible. The characteristic length given for each rung is the scale of the object under study, not of the instrument.

10⁻⁹ m

Analyte

NIR overtone · 1500–2400 nm

Non-invasive glucose is the canonical hard case, and it is hard for reasons that are estimation-theoretic rather than optical. The C–H and O–H combination and overtone bands that carry glucose information sit inside a spectral region overwhelmingly dominated by water absorption, in tissue that scatters strongly and whose optical properties vary with temperature, hydration and perfusion. The analyte's contribution to the measured spectrum is small, and—critically—is not orthogonal to the confounders. Multivariate calibration will happily produce a model with excellent cross-validated performance that is in fact tracking skin temperature.

The rigorous framing is the net analyte signal: the component of the analyte's spectrum orthogonal to the space spanned by every interferent. That quantity, not the raw absorbance, sets the Cramér–Rao floor on the concentration estimate. When it is small, no amount of model capacity rescues you, and the appearance of accuracy in a validation set is evidence of correlated confounding, not of measurement.

The neuropsychology reader will recognise the structure immediately, because fNIRS has the same shape. The modified Beer–Lambert law converts attenuation to chromophore concentration only through a differential pathlength factor that is itself uncertain and subject-specific, and the measured signal is contaminated by superficial scalp haemodynamics that correlate with task in exactly the way that ruins an unwary contrast. Short-separation regression channels are the field's answer, and they are structurally identical to the calibration strip in Figure 2: measure the confounder in-frame rather than assuming it away.

10⁻⁶ – 10⁻¹ m

The one-pixel argument

any band · incoherent measurement

The single-pixel camera is the sharpest statement in the whole review, and it is a theorem before it is a device. The Candès–Romberg–Tao and Donoho results establish that a signal that is K-sparse in some basis can be recovered exactly, with high probability, from on the order of K log(N/K) linear measurements against a measurement ensemble incoherent with that basis — not from N measurements. The Rice single-pixel camera realises this with a digital micromirror array throwing pseudorandom patterns onto one photodiode.

The consequence worth internalising is that resolution is not a property of the detector array. It is a joint property of the measurement ensemble and the prior. A single detector plus a well-chosen set of masks can outperform a megapixel sensor, and a megapixel sensor sampling incoherently can be undersampled by an order of magnitude with no loss on a sparse scene. This is why hyperspectral imaging can afford one exquisite cooled detector scanned across a scene rather than a focal-plane array at every wavelength, and it is the same theorem that licenses undersampled MRI acquisition.

10⁻² – 10⁰ m

The holdable object

X-ray · VNIR/SWIR · low-coherence IR

This is the conservation reader's own rung, and it is the one where the largest number of distinct physical couplings are brought to bear on a single object — which makes it the best place to see that the couplings are what differ and nothing else is.

Macro X-ray fluorescence scanning maps elemental distribution by raster and recovers composition from characteristic emission lines; the inverse problem is peak deconvolution against self-absorption in a layered stack. Reflectance imaging spectroscopy across VNIR and SWIR identifies pigments and binders from reflectance features, and its hard problem is unmixing: a pixel's spectrum is a nonlinear mixture of the components within the scattering volume, which is formally the same ill-posed unmixing that a satellite hyperspectral pipeline performs on a 30-metre ground pixel. Infrared reflectography at roughly 1–2.5 µm exploits the fact that many pigments become effectively transparent in the near infrared while carbon-black underdrawing continues to absorb; the underdrawing was always there, and what changed was the band in which you interrogated it.

Two techniques on this rung obtain their resolution from coherence rather than from aperture, and they are the ones that recur furthest away. Optical coherence tomography gates returns by coherence length, so its axial resolution scales as roughly λ²/Δλ and is decoupled from numerical aperture entirely — which is how micrometre-scale varnish and glaze stratigraphy is resolved non-invasively through a working distance. And speckle-pattern interferometry measures out-of-plane surface displacement to a fraction of a wavelength by differencing phase between two states of thermal or acoustic load, revealing subsurface delamination as a fringe anomaly. Its hard problems are decorrelation and phase unwrapping. Hold that thought for four rungs.

10⁰ – 10¹ m

Habitable

structured light · time of flight

The Folk table that most of this corpus was written for is a camera and a projector sharing a calibrated volume, and its significance is that the illumination is designed. Once you control the probe, sensing becomes a coding problem: structured light projects a known spatial code and reads depth out of its deformation; time-of-flight modulates a known temporal code and reads range out of its phase. Both then face wrapping ambiguity and resolve it with multi-frequency schemes, which is the Chinese remainder theorem doing metrology.

Coded active illumination is exactly what radar pulse compression does with a chirp, and exactly what an interferometer does with a coherent reference arm. The design freedom is the probe waveform, and the payoff is measured in the ambiguity function.

10² – 10³ m

Neighbourhood

LiDAR · distributed acoustic sensing

Airborne and terrestrial LiDAR return a point cloud whose accuracy is dominated not by the ranging precision but by the pose solution: the same geometric-calibration problem as blob.folk, with an IMU and GNSS trajectory in place of a projector matrix. Distributed acoustic sensing turns an existing telecom fibre into a dense strain array by interrogating Rayleigh backscatter, converting infrastructure into an instrument — the purest example in this review of a sensor that exists because someone noticed an already-propagating field carried recoverable information.

10⁵ – 10⁶ m

Region

SAR interferometry · C-band

Here the recurrence becomes literal. InSAR forms the phase difference between two complex synthetic-aperture radar acquisitions of the same ground and recovers line-of-sight displacement at millimetre to centimetre precision over hundreds of kilometres. Its two dominant error sources are temporal and geometric decorrelation, and the tropospheric phase screen — a spatially correlated nuisance term that must be estimated and removed. Its central algorithmic difficulty is two-dimensional phase unwrapping, which is NP-hard in the general weighted case and is attacked with branch cuts, minimum-cost flow, or network programming.

That is, term for term, the panel-painting speckle interferometry problem from three rungs up: differencing phase between two states, fighting decorrelation, removing a correlated nuisance field, and unwrapping. The baseline differs by seven orders of magnitude. The estimator does not differ at all.

10⁷ m

Planet

imaging spectroscopy · satellite gravimetry

Orbital imaging spectrometers do the conservator's pigment-unmixing problem on a planetary raster, with atmospheric correction standing in for varnish. But the instructive instrument at this rung is GRACE, which senses nothing optically at all: it measures the separation between two satellites by microwave ranging, and infers mass redistribution — groundwater depletion, ice loss — from the perturbation of that separation. Imaging is one option for sensing, not a synonym for it. When the coupling of interest is gravitational, the right detector is a ruler between two test masses.

10¹² – 10²¹ m

Celestial body, and beyond

aperture synthesis · km-baseline interferometry

Very-long-baseline interferometry correlates signals from antennas continents apart. By the van Cittert–Zernike theorem, what each baseline measures is one complex visibility — a single Fourier component of the sky brightness distribution — so the array samples the Fourier plane at a sparse, irregular set of points determined by baseline geometry and Earth rotation. Producing an image is therefore not detection but reconstruction from gravely incomplete Fourier data under a regularising prior. Which is the single-pixel-camera theorem again, at 10²⁰ times the scale: the resolution comes from the measurement ensemble, the plausibility of the image comes from the prior, and stating which is which is the entire methodological burden of publishing such an image.

And at the far end, a Michelson interferometer with 4 km arms measuring a strain of order 10⁻²¹, a differential arm-length change some three orders of magnitude below a proton radius, extracted by matched filtering against a bank of modelled waveform templates. The instrument on the conservator's bench measuring varnish stratigraphy and the instrument that detects a black-hole merger are the same optical topology and the same detection statistic. What changed is the arm length, the isolation, and the fact that at that scale the noise floor is set by quantum mechanics rather than by engineering.

05  /  What the theorems give you

On seeing into the truth of things, with the emphasis on into

The framing that prompted this review was Wittgensteinian, and the term deserves more care than it usually gets when it is attached to instrumentation. Both of the obvious readings turn out to cut against the triumphalist version of the thesis, which is what makes them worth stating.

The Tractatus reading is that a representation cannot depict its own form of representation — it can only show it. This is a precise statement about instruments. Every measurement chain in this review has a set of assumptions it cannot itself report on: the differential pathlength factor, the assumed printer gamut, the sparsity basis, the linearity of the mixing model, the tropospheric screen. These do not appear in the output as terms. They appear as the shape of the output. An estimate that arrives without its assumptions attached has lost precisely the information needed to know what it is an estimate of.

The Investigations reading is sharper still, and the neuropsychology reader will have the duck-rabbit to hand. Aspect-seeing is not a further fact about the figure; it is a fact about a practice of looking. A false-colour elemental map is not a fact about a painting until it has been embedded in a practice that says what counts as a pentimento, what counts as a later restoration, what counts as noise. The remarkable thing is not that instruments settle such questions. It is that they reliably produce images which look as though they settle them.

What the mathematics delivers is not the truth of the object. It is a provable statement about what can and cannot be recovered from a given measurement — which is a bound on the sayable, and considerably more useful.

This is why the bounds column in the table above is the load-bearing one. Nyquist tells you that an aliased component is gone, not merely degraded, and that no reconstruction will honestly return it. The Cramér–Rao bound tells you the variance floor for any unbiased estimator given your Fisher information, so that a reported precision below it is a claim about your prior and not about your instrument. The restricted isometry property tells you when sparse recovery is exact and, by its absence, when a plausible reconstruction is a hallucination with good posterior support. These theorems do not tell you what is there. They tell you what your apparatus has made it impossible to know, which is the only form of epistemic humility that can be checked by someone else.

The corresponding discipline is unglamorous and it is what the small artifacts in section 02 accidentally demonstrate. Put the reference in the frame. Encode the scale in the artifact. Size the redundancy to the channel you will actually traverse rather than the one you designed for. Keep rectification separable from demodulation so each can be falsified alone. And when a model performs well, ask what it would also predict if the analyte were absent, because the steganography tool's payload survived every transformation except the one it would really meet, and the glucose literature is thirty years of models that were tracking something else.

06  /  Open threads

Where the small artifacts would have to grow

  1. Perspective rectification before demodulation. The page-frame decoder currently assumes an axis-aligned raster and defers deskew to a vision layer that does not yet exist. Closing that gap turns a screenshot codec into a genuine document scanner, and the homography machinery already exists in blob.folk.
  2. A code matched to the lossy channel. The steganographic encoder writes to the LSB plane, which JPEG is licensed to discard. Moving the payload into mid-frequency DCT coefficients with spread-spectrum modulation would survive recompression, and would make the capacity-versus-robustness trade-off explicit rather than implicit.
  3. Reed–Solomon in place of repetition. The page frame repeats a CRC-guarded packet until the band is full and accepts the first copy that verifies. That is a rate-1/n repetition code, which is the least efficient way to spend redundancy. The QR vendoring already in the repository provides the codec.
  4. A measured channel model. Every choice in the frame — palette, cell size, band thickness — is currently justified by argument. Characterising the actual print-and-photograph channel would turn those into a measured symbol error rate and let the code rate be derived rather than guessed. This is the step that separates a demonstration from an instrument, at every scale on the ladder.
