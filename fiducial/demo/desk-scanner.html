Rulercross · desk scanner · scale + pose from pixels

Several rulers on a desk. One camera. Recover each one's data, true size, and pose.

Rulercross markers of different paper sizes are scattered across a tilted desk with the handwritten cards as distractors. The scanner finds each bullseye, traces its arms, decodes the colour data, and — because the marker's physical geometry is known — recovers true scale (mm/px), 3-DoF position & heading, and full 6-DoF pose. Nothing is read from the scene description; everything comes from the rendered pixels.

After Johnny Chung Lee's structured-light & light-sensor pose recovery — here on a plain 2D camera, from print.

How the true scale comes back. Each marker prints its own physical long-arm length L into the CMY cells. The scanner solves a homography from the five known points (centre + four arm tips) in normalised marker units, samples the cells through that homography to read L in millimetres, then converts the homography's Jacobian into mm/px at the marker. Four arm tips (non-collinear) are enough to also decompose the homography with the camera intrinsics into a full rotation and translation — the tilt columns you see per row. The bullseye and long/short-arm asymmetry with a red North pip make the frame’s origin and heading unambiguous, which is why this reads pose where a 1-D barcode cannot.
