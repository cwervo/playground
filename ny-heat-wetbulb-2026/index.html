The Wet-Bulb Gap

You asked for heat deaths by age cohort for this summer, matched to humidity and the wet-bulb depression. The death data does not exist yet — but the exposure data does, and it already separates the cohorts.

A · Observed B · Published research C · Derived here Not yet published

New York City's heat mortality is counted once a year, in arrears. The Health Department's 2026 Heat-Related Mortality Report, published this June, covers deaths through 2025 — 21 heat-stress deaths, the highest tally in a decade, 19 of them in a single four-day June heat wave. Summer 2026's toll, and its age breakdown, will not appear until the 2027 report.

What exists for this summer is a running count of three: the Office of Chief Medical Examiner ruled three deaths accidental hyperthermia during the early-July heat wave, all at home. No ages were released. Anyone showing you a 2026 cohort table is modelling, not reporting.

So this brief does the match the other way around. Every age cohort has a published critical wet-bulb limit — the wet-bulb temperature above which the body can no longer shed heat at rest. Those limits are cohort-specific and measured, not modelled. Summer 2026's observed heat and humidity can be tested against them directly.

The finding

Summer 2026 crossed the older-adult limit repeatedly. It never came near the young-adult one.

At the 79 °F dew point New York actually recorded on 7 August, every air temperature between 82 and 94 °F produces a wet bulb at or above the older-adult critical limit — and none of them come within 2 °C of the young-adult limit.

82–94 °F is not an arbitrary window. It is the band the Health Department identifies as producing the large majority of the city's heat deaths: days that are hot but unremarkable, that trigger no warning and no headline.

Cohorts

What is published by age, and what is not

The Health Department publishes the shape of the age gradient — lowest rates at 20 and under, highest at 60 and over — but not per-cohort counts. One New York relative risk is published with a confidence interval. The critical-limit column is the part that is measured per cohort, and it is the column that does the work here.

Headroom is the wet-bulb depression, air temperature minus wet-bulb temperature. Note the last two columns diverging: between a 70 °F and a 79 °F dew point the heat index rises by under 11 °F, but the share of the band that is physiologically uncompensable for an older adult goes from none of it to all of it.

What this is not

An earlier version of this analysis allocated the ~500 annual heat deaths across cohorts by multiplying population by baseline mortality by (RR − 1). It returned 96% of deaths to the 65+ group — more concentrated than any published figure — because it stacked four modelled assumptions on top of each other. It was removed rather than shipped with a caveat. No per-cohort death count is asserted anywhere on this page.

Method

Wet bulb via Stull (2011), which reproduces the paper's worked example (25 °C, 50% RH → 18.0 °C) exactly. Relative humidity from dew point via Magnus–Tetens. Where only a heat index was reported, the NWS Rothfusz regression is inverted numerically across plausible air temperatures to give a range, not a point. The code is in the repository beside this page and regenerates every number here.

What would make this exact

Two sources would replace the modelled parts with observations, and both were unreachable from this session's network: the NYC Environment & Health Data Portal's heat-report appendix, which carries the per-cohort mortality tables, and the New York State Heat Risk and Illness Dashboard, which carries near-real-time heat-related ED visits by age group for this summer. Hourly station observations from NOAA for KNYC and KLGA would replace the five reported events with a full 92-day wet-bulb series.
