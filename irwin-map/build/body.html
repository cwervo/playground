Robert Irwin Within 300 Miles

Irwin spent sixty years making work that only exists where you stand in it — a scrim in a particular room, a steel line cut into one hillside. So the honest question is not what he made, but what you can still get to. Thirteen places inside the circle, sorted by how you'd reach them.

How this was made

Distance is great-circle mileage from Times Square (40.758° N, 73.986° W), computed per county centroid against the US Census county boundaries; 282 counties fall inside 300 miles. The map is an Albers equal-area conic projection with standard parallels at 37° and 44°. Travel notes are typical trips, not live schedules — check the operator before you go, and check with the institution before you travel for a specific work: most of these are collection holdings whose display rotates. Only Dia Beacon and Wellesley are permanently sited and reliably there.

Color

The six route hues were selected by search against a colour-vision-deficiency validator and pass every gate on all 15 pairs, in both light and dark themes: worst-case CVD separation ΔE 10.1 (target ≥ 8) and worst-case normal-vision separation ΔE 15.7 (floor ≥ 15), simulated for protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia. Each mode also carries a distinct dash pattern, and every route is directly labelled, so the encoding never rests on hue alone. The county shading is a single neutral ramp on purpose — it keeps the entire colour budget for the lines. Marker shape, not colour, carries whether a work is sited, held in a collection, or gone.
